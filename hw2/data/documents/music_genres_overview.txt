1. CORE DEFINITION
A music genre is a classification category defined by shared sonic characteristics: tempo (BPM), instrumentation, rhythm patterns, vocal style, production approach, and harmonic language. Genres function as heuristic reference points for algorithmic tagging, playlist routing, and audio analysis. They are fluid; classification often requires identifying a primary structural framework and secondary borrowed elements.

2. GENRE CLASSIFICATION SCHEMA
When analyzing, tagging, or generating music, use the following sonic fingerprints to classify the primary genre:

- POP: 100-130 BPM. Traits: Strong melodic hooks, polished production, standard verse-chorus structure. Instruments: Synthesizers, programmed drums, layered top-line vocals.
- HIP-HOP: 70-100 BPM (or 130-160 half-time). Traits: Rhythmic vocal delivery, heavy bass focus. Instruments: 808s, sampled breaks, trap hi-hats.
- R&B: 60-110 BPM. Traits: Smooth vocal-centric production, syncopated grooves, lush harmonies. Instruments: Keyboards, bass guitar, layered vocal stacks.
- ROCK: 110-140 BPM. Traits: Loud-soft dynamics, live-band acoustic feel. Instruments: Amplified electric guitar, bass guitar, acoustic drum kit.
- ELECTRONIC / EDM: 120-150+ BPM. Traits: Production-first focus, repetitive loop structures, energy arcs (build-and-drop). Instruments: Synthesizers, drum machines, sidechain compression.
- COUNTRY: 90-130 BPM. Traits: Narrative/storytelling lyrics, acoustic resonance, twang. Instruments: Acoustic guitar, pedal steel guitar, fiddle.
- JAZZ: 80-200+ BPM. Traits: High harmonic complexity, extended chords, improvisation, swung or complex rhythms. Instruments: Horns, upright bass, brushed drums, piano.
- FOLK: 80-120 BPM. Traits: Simple harmonic structures, narrative focus. Instruments: Pure acoustic (guitar, banjo, mandolin).
- METAL: 100-200+ BPM. Traits: Extreme amplification, aggressive vocal delivery, complex rhythmic arrangements. Instruments: Down-tuned distorted guitars, double-kick drums.
- REGGAE: 60-90 BPM. Traits: Offbeat rhythm emphasis (skank), laid-back groove, heavy bass. Instruments: Bass, rhythm guitar, one-drop drum pattern.
- LATIN: 80-130 BPM. Traits: Syncopated clave rhythm patterns, percussion-heavy. Instruments: Congas, timbales, horns, acoustic guitar.
- CLASSICAL: Highly variable BPM. Traits: Composed notation, extreme dynamic range (volume). Instruments: Orchestral strings, woodwinds, brass, piano.

3. SUBGENRE MAPPING
Subgenres represent specific mutations or historical iterations of the primary genres:
- Pop Subgenres: Synth-pop (1980s synths), Indie Pop (lo-fi production), Dance-Pop (4-on-the-floor beat), Art Pop (experimental structure).
- Hip-Hop Subgenres: Trap (808 bass, rapid hi-hats), Boom-bap (punchy sampled drums), Drill (sliding 808s, aggressive delivery), Lo-fi (relaxed, sample-heavy).
- R&B Subgenres: Neo-soul (jazz/live instrument influence), Contemporary (modern electronic production).
- Rock Subgenres: Indie, Punk (fast, stripped-down), Hard Rock (heavier distortion), Post-punk (angular, atmospheric).
- Electronic Subgenres: House (120-130 BPM, 4-on-the-floor), Techno (repetitive, industrial), Drum & Bass (160-180 BPM, breakbeats), Dubstep (half-time, wobble bass), Ambient (textural, beatless).
- Country Subgenres: Traditional (acoustic, 3-chord), Country Pop (arena hooks), Americana (roots/folk blend).
- Jazz Subgenres: Bebop (fast, virtuosic), Cool Jazz (understated), Fusion (electronic/rock blend).

4. OPERATIONAL HEURISTICS FOR THE AGENT
When evaluating music strategy, production, or metadata, apply the following rules:

- RULE 1 (Tagging Strategy): Streaming recommendation algorithms require precise tagging. Mistagging a track (e.g., labeling Folk as Electronic) causes algorithmic misrouting, resulting in low listener retention and negative algorithmic penalization.
- RULE 2 (Analyzing Blends): Successful genre blending consists of a Primary Genre (which dictates the rhythmic and structural framework) and a Secondary Genre (which provides color, such as harmonic language or specific instrumentation). Example: "Pop structure with Jazz harmony."
- RULE 3 (Production Constraints): A track must maintain the core sonic conventions of its genre to be classified as such. Altering the fundamental frequency balance (e.g., removing the low-end bass from a Hip-Hop track) reclassifies the audio.
