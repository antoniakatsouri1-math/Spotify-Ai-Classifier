AI-Generated Music Detection: Characteristics, Challenges, and Methods
Domain: Spotify Audio Feature Analysis for Human vs AI Classification

OVERVIEW

The rise of AI music generation systems — including tools such as Suno, Udio, MusicLM, and MusicGen — has created a growing need for automated classifiers that can distinguish AI-generated tracks from those created by human artists. Streaming platforms, rights management organisations, and artists all have an interest in detecting the origin of a musical track. A reliable classifier enables automated labelling, rights routing, and integrity checks without requiring human review.

The problem is non-trivial because AI systems are increasingly trained to mimic human-produced audio profiles, making naive heuristics ineffective. Machine learning classifiers trained on audio features offer a more robust approach.

KEY AUDIO FEATURE DIFFERENCES: AI vs HUMAN TRACKS

Research and dataset analysis consistently identify the following patterns:

1. ENERGY AND ACOUSTICNESS
AI-generated tracks tend to have simultaneously high energy and low acousticness. This combination — captured by the engineered feature energy/acousticness ratio — is one of the strongest signals for AI origin. Human music shows a much wider distribution across this space, including many high-acousticness, lower-energy tracks (singer-songwriter, folk, classical).

2. INSTRUMENTALNESS
AI generation systems frequently produce highly instrumental content, particularly in ambient, electronic, and background music genres where there is high commercial demand but lower artistic scrutiny. High instrumentalness (>0.5) is more common in AI-generated tracks.

3. DANCEABILITY AND VALENCE
AI music production systems often optimise simultaneously for danceability and positive valence, since these features are associated with high engagement metrics on streaming platforms. The product of danceability × valence (danceability_valence_product) captures co-optimisation behaviour that individual features alone cannot fully express.

4. LOUDNESS
AI mastering tends to produce more loudness-normalised output. The loudness_positive feature (loudness + 60, shifting the negative dB scale to a non-negative range) helps expose this pattern in combination with energy.

5. LIVENESS
AI-generated tracks have near-zero liveness scores, as they are by definition studio-generated without any live audience. While many human studio recordings also have low liveness, very low liveness combined with high instrumentalness is a stronger AI signal.

6. DURATION AND SHORT-FORM CONTENT
AI generation is frequently used for short-form content (jingles, background music, short clips). The short_form binary feature (1 if duration is below a threshold) captures this tendency.

MACHINE LEARNING APPROACHES

Binary classification (Human=0, AI-Generated=1) using Spotify audio features has been shown to achieve high discriminative power (ROC-AUC > 0.95) with tree-based and neural network models.

Most discriminative features (from feature importance analysis):
1. instrumentalness
2. acousticness
3. liveness
4. energy_acousticness_ratio (engineered)
5. energy

Neural networks with standardised inputs (StandardScaler) and dropout regularisation are particularly effective, as the feature interactions (e.g. energy × acousticness) are non-linear and benefit from learned representations.

CLASS IMBALANCE
In real-world datasets, human-made tracks typically outnumber AI-generated tracks (roughly 2:1 ratio). This imbalance is handled via class_weight='balanced' in tree-based models and weighted loss functions in neural networks, ensuring the minority class (AI) is not overwhelmed during training.

WHY THIS MATTERS

The ability to detect AI-generated music at scale has several practical applications:
- Streaming platforms can flag or label AI content for transparency
- Rights management organisations can route royalty flows correctly
- Human artists benefit from differentiated discovery and promotion
- Regulators and industry bodies can monitor the volume of AI content on platforms

As AI music systems become more sophisticated, classifiers must be continuously retrained on new data to remain effective against increasingly human-like AI audio profiles.
